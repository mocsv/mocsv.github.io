*Aimed at those from the UK, however, most if not all steps are still applicable (with slight changes) for other parts of the world.*

This guide will run you through a power outage from **Minute One**, with what to do, and when and how to do it.

## Minute One

So you think your power has gone out. You suddenly find yourself buttering your toast in the dark or getting blasted with freezing cold water in a pitch black shower.

Minute one is vital. 

Don't go boiling beans on the camping stove in your attic only to find out you just needed to flip a switch in your fuse box...

- Stay calm.
- Check your fuse box to see if the outage is self inflicted.
- Look out the window at your neighbours and streetlights to see if they are in the dark.

If it's a fuse issue your can *partially* relax as most of this guide is no longer relevant to you. If you're not alone in the dark we can move on to the next four minutes.

## Minute Five

- If you have mobile service check for power outage updates online.
- Call 105 (from the UK) to access your electricity distribution network where they can update you on the status. If you have medical devices that need electricity they can also put you on a priority list or help you get additional support.
- Do you experience outages in your area regularly? Has there been a recent storm? Logically think of why the power could be out. 

**It's a lot more likely to be a tree than the start of a zombie apocalypse.**

Most local outages are restored within a few minutes so no need to run around the house with the sleeping bags and glow sticks just yet.

If it's a large scale outage it's likely that:

- Lights and appliances are instantly dark.
- Wifi is down.
- Mobile networks may still be running on backup.
- There's public confusion as people try and get a grasp on the scale of the outage.
- Trains have stopped. Traffic lights may fail and multiple alarm systems will be triggered.

## Minute Ten

Okay, so your power is still out. You've checked online, called 105 or stared out your window into your neighbours kitchen. There's a confirmed outage in your area.

At this stage you want to get a bit prepared.

- Gather your emergency supplies - *see recommended item list*
- Unplug any appliances or devices from sockets in case of a surge when power is returned, which could damage them.
- Use your emergency torch/headlamp to navigate if its dark.
- Leave a light on to track when power is restored.
- If you fancy, you can make a note of the time so you can track how long the power outage lasts.

## Hour One

You're walking around your house with a headlight on and frantically refreshing your phone news feed, if you have service that is.

You have your emergency supplies in an easy to reach location and have secured your devices from a potential surge. Now you can prepare for the next few hours.

- Do not open the fridge or freezer. Items in your fridge can remain cold for around 4 hours if not opened. A full freezer can last up to 48 hours. 24 if its half full. Therefore, it is essential to only open them if necessary.
- Put your mobile phone and devices in power saving mode. Consider not trying to use them for Instagram reels and instead turn off WiFi, Bluetooth and location services to conserve battery.
- Keep yourself and those around you entertained and in high spirits. It can be very handy to have a pack or cards, games, glow sticks and fairy lights in your emergency supplies if you have young children.

If it's a large scale outage it's likely that:

- Emergency services are overwhelmed with calls.
- Public transport is down.
- Backup generators in hospitals etc. have started kicking in.

## Day One

You're doing great.

Now it's mostly a waiting game. At this point you should be well in to your *Emergency Kit* supplies. You have the tools necessary to eat, drink, rest and recharge.

- Throw out any perishables that have been in the fridge longer than 4 hours.
- If it's winter ensure you are using your supplies to stay warm and comfortable. Consider moving all family members in to one room to conserve heat.
- Use your radio or mobile devices to stay updated on the situation.
- If you need to drive to get more supplies be mindful of your fuel levels and shopping conditions. It's likely people are panic buying and chaos is to be expected. Many ATM machines will be down and any open stores will be cash only. Fuel stations will also stop pumping after a few hours so it's always handy to stay half full.

If it's a large scale outage it's likely that:

- A national emergency is declared. Government is likely to issue a public statement on the cause of the outage.
- Radio becomes vital communication with other methods offline.
- Homes and shops refrigerators are no longer refrigerating.
- Flights are mostly grounded.
- Hospitals are in a critical state relying on backup power.
- Slight chaos may begin with potential crime increasing especially with shoplifting. Police will be preparing for disorder.

## Day Two Onwards

This would be where a more 'US end of the world prepper' mindset could come in handy. Not the 'mad gun' ones but more the 'few weeks worth of food and water for the family, larger backup power or generator and general knowledge of basic first aid and survival' ones.

If it's a large scale outage it's likely that:

- Supermarkets will run out of stock. Shelves will be empty.
- Cash will be used everywhere and those without will not be able to purchase goods.
- Power banks will become one of the most valuable items.
- Petrol stations will close.
- People may try and leave the area.
- Households will start to lose access to clean water.
- Emergency shelter, food and water stations may be set up.
- Disinformation will spread. Panic will rise. Crime will increase.
- There would be a critical national emergency.

If the UK experiences a week long large scale power outage it will be nothing less than chaos. However, the possibility of that happening is very, very, *very* slim.

---

The likelihood of there being an outage under 24 hours is a lot more likely and in some cases almost guaranteed to happen for many people in many places. 

Whether it's minutes or hours the general knowledge and basic supplies to get you and your family through a power outage of any scale is essential. If anything it will allow you to rest easy, knowing that if the worst happened you and your loved ones would be a step ahead.
